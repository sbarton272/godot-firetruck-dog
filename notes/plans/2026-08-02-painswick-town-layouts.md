## What every option has

- **Real streets** at real proportions — you can walk any of these SVGs on
  the actual OSM map and it will match.
- **St Mary's church** appears in every layout (labelled, with the † cross
  symbol) as a shared reference point.
- **Multiple intersections and named lanes** so there's real routing
  choice, not one road.
- **Dense buildings** along every street — the actual Painswick building
  footprints, not sampled placeholders.
- **Fields, waterways, walls and hedges** where OSM records them, giving
  the boundary/farmland flavour asked for.
- **Truck start (S)** and **fire target (F)** picked to force a route
  through the interesting geometry.
- **Compass, 100 m scale bar, legend** on every map.

## Research summary — what Painswick actually looks like

Painswick sits on a hilltop over the Painswick stream valley, on the old
Gloucester–Bisley–Cirencester road, built in soft grey Cotswold stone. The
old town is a tight knot of narrow, kinked streets rather than a grid:

- A single main thoroughfare (today the A46) runs **N→S** through the
  village: Cheltenham Rd / Gloucester St / New St / Stroud Rd. It's not
  straight — it kinks around the churchyard.
- **Bisley Street** — the oldest street (14th c), medieval cottages, runs
  along the ridge.
- **New Street** — cut in the 15th c parallel and west of Bisley, pulled
  the town's focus westward; home to the "oldest post office in England".
- **St Mary's Church** — dominant landmark, Grade I listed, surrounded by
  the famous churchyard of ~99 clipped yew trees.
- **Market Cross / market place** — sits at the SW angle where Bisley St
  meets Friday St, just west of the churchyard.
- **Victoria Street** — short N–S link along the north edge of the
  churchyard, from New St to St Mary's St.
- **Tibbiwell Lane** — Bisley St's continuation, drops steeply SE downhill
  toward St Tabitha's Well.
- **Vicarage Street** — spreads east/downhill into the valley toward the
  Painswick Stream and old mill.
- **Fields all around**, hedged and drystone-walled: Ham Butts west of
  town, cricket/rugby fields (the Recreation Ground) north-east, and open
  sheep pasture climbing to **Painswick Beacon** (Iron Age hillfort) NE.

## Option 1 — Town Centre Core (around St Mary's)

**bbox ~434 × 423 m** centred on St Mary's church.

The tight medieval knot: Bisley St and New St running N–S in parallel, the
churchyard triangle between them, Victoria St and Friday St linking east–
west, Tibbiwell Lane peeling off SE downhill. Town Hall just north of the
churchyard. Buildings are the densest of any option — real Cotswold cottages
packed shoulder to shoulder along every lane.

- Truck start: on New St approaching the market from the south.
- Fire: cottage at the Vicarage St / Hollyhock Lane corner, forcing a
  route through the market / around the churchyard.

**Feel:** the most authentic Painswick, biggest maze of tight lanes. Best
if you want the "historic village core" identity.

---

## Option 2 — North Y-Gateway (Cheltenham & Gloucester meet)

**bbox ~445 × 390 m** just north of the church.

The A46 Y-junction where Cheltenham Rd meets Gloucester St, with a proper
network of residential loops: Butt Green, Pullens Rd, Hollyhock Lane. The
Croft Primary School and its grounds anchor the west edge. Sloping down S
into the top of New St / the church area.

- Truck start: coming in from the north on Cheltenham Rd.
- Fire: cottage near The Croft on the west side, so the truck has to pick
  a side-loop off the main Y.

**Feel:** clear "entering the village" story with one dominant fork
choice, then a suburban lane network. Second-easiest to navigate.

---

## Option 3 — East Slope to Painswick Stream

**bbox ~547 × 390 m** running east from the church.

Vicarage St and the eastern lanes dropping from the ridge to the Painswick
Stream at the east edge. St Mary's on the west edge (reference). The
Recreation Ground (cricket / football pitches) fills the north. Small
lanes and dead ends throughout the east.

- Truck start: at the church end, west edge.
- Fire: cottage / old mill down by the stream on the east edge — forces a
  long descent through the lane network.

**Feel:** strong "downhill" identity, real waterway on the far side, the
sports fields make a large open landmark. Nice mix of tight town and open
green space.

---

## Option 4 — Full A46 Spine (Cheltenham → Stroud Rd)

**bbox ~479 × 890 m** — a long thin north-south strip covering the entire
A46 through Painswick.

The whole spine. Cheltenham Rd at the top, Y-junction with Gloucester St,
past the church halfway down, out via Stroud Rd at the bottom. Every side
lane in the village stubs off left or right. Long map — biggest driving
distance of any option.

- Truck start: north end on Cheltenham Rd.
- Fire: cottage at Stroud Rd south end.

**Feel:** cinematic drive-through-the-whole-village. Route decisions come
from *which* side lane to shortcut through, not from spine choice. Best
for a longer level or a "race the fire truck" mode.

---

## Option 5 — South-East Knot (max maze)

**bbox ~479 × 500 m** covering the densest lane tangle in the village.

Tibbiwell Lane, Hale Lane, Kemps Lane, Knapp Lane, Ticklestone Lane, top
of Kingsmill Lane — a proper knot of narrow, winding residential lanes on
the SE slope. St Mary's anchors the NW corner as your reference; the
Painswick Stream trickles down the E side.

- Truck start: by the church at the NW corner.
- Fire: cottage down at the Knapp / Ticklestone junction at the SE
  corner — forces navigation through the whole lane tangle.

**Feel:** maximum maze. Every route to the fire has multiple valid
choices; getting turned around is easy. Best if maze-navigation is the
primary gameplay hook.

---

## Decision: Option 1 selected

We're going with **Option 1 — Town Centre Core**.

The playable simplification of it is here:
[`2026-08-02-painswick-maps/2026-08-02-painswick-game-plan.svg`](2026-08-02-painswick-maps/2026-08-02-painswick-game-plan.svg).

Game plan summary (see the SVG for the map + full entity list):

- Playfield ≈ **150 × 150 game units** (map draws at 6 SVG px = 1 unit).
- **6 streets** — New St + Bisley St (parallel N–S spines), Victoria St + Friday St (E–W links), Tibbiwell Ln (SE exit), Vicarage St (E exit to the fire).
- Roads use **gentle 22-unit-wide curves** — no perpendicular hairpins, so the truck's turning radius stays playable.
- **~28 houses** (three roof-style variants), **St Mary's Church** with a ring of ~14 yews inside the churchyard, and a distinct **Town Hall** on Victoria St.
- **Market Cross** at the Friday St / New St corner as a landmark obstacle.
- **7 parked cars** rotated per street, **10 lampposts** at junctions/market/church/exits, **3 mailboxes** + **3 trash cans** scattered as clutter.
- **~14 fence segments** along the N/W/S/E field boundary (drystone-wall gaps).
- **StartPoint** at the south end of New St (a small fire-station pad).
- **GoalArea (fire)** at the Vicarage St NE cottage — forces routing through the market, past the church, and out the east exit.
